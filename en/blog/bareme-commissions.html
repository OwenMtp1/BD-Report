💶 Compensation

Building a commission grid your BDRs actually understand

≈ 6 min read · BD Report team

A good commission plan isn't the one that pays the most — it's the one a rep can compute in their head. If they don't understand how they earn, they won't steer their activity toward what matters. Here are the principles that keep coming up in teams that nail their SDR/BDR comp plan.

1. Two structures, chosen clearly

There are two main models for an SDR/BDR. The first — the most common — pays a fixed amount per milestone (SQL, held meeting, accepted opportunity) plus a few activity bonuses. The second, less frequent, pays a percentage of pipeline generated or revenue closed. Fixed-per-milestone is more legible and fits the SDR role better; percentage aligns more with the business but makes pay less predictable.

2. The right base / variable balance

The B2B SaaS market has converged on a 70/30 mix: 70% base, 30% variable — the most common balance between security and motivation for an SDR. On the target side, aim for a quota-to-OTE ratio of 5:1 to 8:1 — pipeline generated worth 5 to 8× the fully-loaded cost of the role.

2026 benchmark (US B2B SaaS): median OTE around $85K, 70/30 mix. Adapt the amounts to your market, but keep the ratios.

3. The three-brick bonus

A rising standard: a three-component variable that follows the lead across its life and rewards quality, not just volume:

  • Per held meeting — rewards activity and booking.
  • Per accepted opportunity (SQL/SAL) — rewards real qualification.
  • Per closed-won deal — aligns the SDR with final revenue.

Splitting half the target on qualified opportunities and half on revenue avoids the "vanity SQL" that never converts.

4. Accelerators and guardrails

If the bonus is properly tied to pipeline, offer accelerators beyond 100% attainment to reward overperformance — an accelerator cap around 150% is a healthy marker. Build a ramp for new hires, and avoid caps that kill motivation the moment quota is hit.

5. The golden rule: understandable

The best grid drives the right behaviors and discourages the wrong ones — while staying simple enough to compute on the back of a napkin. Version it: a grid change must never rewrite bonuses already earned, or you break trust.

In BD Report: the grid is set by headcount bracket × lead source, each bonus is frozen when the lead turns SQL (the 15th rule sets the payout month), and a weighted forecast estimates upcoming bonuses.

Key takeaways

  • Pick a clear structure (usually fixed-per-milestone) and a ~70/30 mix.
  • Pay for quality: held meeting, accepted opportunity, closed-won deal.
  • Accelerate overperformance, version the bonuses, keep it all head-computable.
