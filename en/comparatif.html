Spreadsheet, CRM, or BD Report?

The two real alternatives to BD Report aren't other prospecting tools: they're the spreadsheet you already keep and the CRM you already pay for. Here's what each one does, what it doesn't, and why the answer is often “all three”.

This page is written for someone who is hesitating. We don't claim a CRM is bad — it isn't — nor that a spreadsheet is useless: it let thousands of teams track their activity before anything else existed. We're saying where each one stops.

📊 What a spreadsheet does, and where it stops

A spreadsheet is unbeatable on one count: it costs nothing and does exactly what you tell it. For one person tracking thirty accounts it is often the right tool, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest.

It stops in three places, always the same ones. One formula edited mid-year rewrites the past: nobody can say which grid applied when the deal landed, and the end of the month gets negotiated instead of simply read. Two people cannot work in it without colliding: you end up with one version per rep and a manual reconciliation every week. And it reminds you of nothing: a no-show to rebook only exists if someone remembers it.

The sign a spreadsheet has hit its limit: someone spends more than an hour a week making it agree with reality.

🗂️ What a CRM does, and where it stops

A CRM tracks accounts and deals across their whole life, often over several years and several contacts. It is an enterprise tool built for the full sales cycle, and nothing here proposes to replace it.

It stops where the BDR's job begins. A CRM does not compute variable pay: the commission plan lives in a separate file kept by HR or the manager. It does not know what a no-show cost, because a no-show isn't a sales stage. And above all, it never shows a rep what they will make this month — which is the only question that will make them open a reporting tool every morning.

The sign a CRM isn't enough: your BDRs fill it in because they're told to, not because they get something out of it.

🤝 Why the answer is often “all three”

Two rows deserve a pause, because they say the opposite of what a sales page normally says. BD Report is not your company's source of truth: if you have a CRM, it stays. And BD Report is not free once the beta ends, where a spreadsheet is free forever.

That is why the HubSpot integration exists: companies, contacts, meetings and tasks go into your portal, and both tools tell the same story. The CRM keeps the sales cycle; BD Report holds the BDR's daily activity and their pay, which the CRM will never model.

Keep the spreadsheet if…

You work alone, track fewer than fifty accounts, and your bonus fits in one multiplication. You need nothing else, and we won't sell you otherwise.

Keep the CRM if…

Your cycles run past six months, several teams touch the same accounts, and your leadership needs a single source. You'll keep it anyway.

Add BD Report if…

You have BDRs on variable pay, and “how much do I make this month” is currently settled by email or in a meeting. That is exactly the gap this product fills.
